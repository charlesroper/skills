# Guardian Style Sub-Editor

Copy-edit and convert existing text to adhere strictly to The Guardian Style Guide while preserving the author's original voice, structure, and argument.

## Auxiliary Resources

The complete, unabridged Guardian and Observer Style Guide reference is split alphabetically into four volumes under `references/`:
- `references/guardian-style-guide-a-f.md` (Entries A to F)
- `references/guardian-style-guide-g-l.md` (Entries G to L)
- `references/guardian-style-guide-m-r.md` (Entries M to R)
- `references/guardian-style-guide-s-z.md` (Entries S to Z)

Consult the relevant volume using your file-reading tool (such as `read_file` or `skills:get_skill_resources`) whenever dealing with specific terms, obscure proper nouns, diplomatic or religious naming conventions, foreign placenames, or whenever a definitive house verdict is needed on edge cases not covered by core rules.

## When to Use

  - The user provides existing text and asks to convert, edit, sub-edit, or proofread it into Guardian style.
  - The user asks to check a draft against Guardian house rules, UK editorial standards, or specific Guardian word choices.
  - The user wants a detailed breakdown of style corrections made to a piece of writing.

## Copy-Editing Workflow

1.  **Preserve Voice and Structure**: Do not rewrite sentences for tone unless requested. Focus strictly on orthography, typography, capitalisation, grammar/agreement, punctuation, number formatting, and Guardian lexical preferences.
2.  **Targeted Reference Retrieval**: If an edit hinges on a specific entry, unusual proper noun, foreign placename, or contentious term, look up the corresponding volume in `references/` (`a-f`, `g-l`, `m-r`, or `s-z`) using your file-reading tool (such as `read_file` or `skills:get_skill_resources`) to verify the exact ruling.
3.  **Apply Core Mechanics**:
      - **Spelling**: Use British English with `-ise`/`-yse` endings (`organise`, `realise`, `analyse`, `paralyse`), `-our` (`colour`, `behaviour`, `glamour` but `glamorous`), `-re` (`centre`, `theatre`, `metre`, `litre`), `-ae-`/`-oe-` (`anaesthetic`, `caesarean`, `foetus`, `manoeuvre`, `oestrogen`, `paediatric`, `diarrhoea`, though `encyclopedia` and `medieval` drop the `a`), and doubled `l` (`travelled`, `cancelling`, `modelling`, `labelled`, `appal`/`appalling`, `enthral`/`enthralling`, `instil`/`instilling`, but `enrol`/`enrolling`/`enrolment` and `install`/`instalment`/`installation`). Drop accents on anglicised French/loan words (`cafe`, `chateau`, `cliche`, `debacle`, `elite`, `facade`, `fete`, `matinee`, `melee`, `naive`, `premiere`, `risque`, `role`, `soiree`), retaining them only on `exposé`, `lamé`, `paté`, `résumé`, `roué`, and unassimilated foreign names/phrases.
      - **Down-Style Capitalisation & Titles**:
          - **Titles vs Job Descriptions**: Capitalise formal titles immediately before a name (`President Barack Obama`, `Pope Francis`, `King Charles III`, `Queen Camilla`, `Prince William`, `Prof Adam Amara`, `Dr Jane Smith`), but lowercase them when standing alone (`the US president, Barack Obama,`, `the pope`, `the king`, `the duke`). Lowercase job descriptions in all positions (`prime minister`, `chancellor`, `home secretary`, `chief executive`, `director`, `chief constable`, `chief rabbi`, `archbishop of Canterbury`). Never use a job description as a false title before a name without an article: write `Keir Starmer, the prime minister,` or `the prime minister, Keir Starmer,` (never `prime minister Keir Starmer`). Use commas around the name if only one person holds the post (`the Labour leader, Keir Starmer,`), and no commas if more than one person fits the description (`the former Labour leader Neil Kinnock`).
          - **Institutions, Parties, and Places**: Lowercase `government`, `parliament`, and `cabinet` in all contexts (except `Cabinet Office`). Capitalise full official names of government departments, agencies, and universities in English-speaking countries (`Home Office`, `Ministry of Justice`, `Department of Defense`, `Crown Prosecution Service`, `Durham University`), but lowercase paraphrased/generic references (`justice ministry`, `the department`, `the university`), university departments (`Durham University department of history`), parliamentary committees/inquiries (`trade and industry select committee`, `Jenkins report`), and generic parts of local institutions (`Wandsworth council`, `St Nicolas church`, `Great Ormond Street children's hospital`, `Ripon grammar school`, `Liverpool John Lennon airport`). Always lowercase `party` in political parties (`Labour party`, `Conservative party`, `Green party`, `Democratic party`, except `Your Party`).
          - **Nature, Time, and Geography**: Lowercase seasons (`spring`, `autumn`), compass points (`the north`, `south-east`, `northern England`, `west Africa`, `the west`, `western Europe`) unless part of an official administrative or geopolitical name (`South Africa`, `North Korea`, `the West Midlands`, `East Anglia`), geological/geographical features (`Sydney harbour`, `Bondi beach`, `Solsbury hill`, `the Channel`, `new year`), and common names of flora, fauna, and dinosaurs (`wild service tree`, `great tit`, `monarch butterfly`, `alsatian`, `triceratops`), capitalising only proper nouns within them (`Dartford warbler`, `English longhorn`). Keep scientific names in italics with an initial capital for the genus and lowercase for the species (`*Sorbus torminalis*`, `*Escherichia coli*`, shortened with no full point as `*E coli*`).
      - **Titles of Works & Italics**: Strip italics and quotation marks from titles of books, films, television programmes, radio shows, podcasts, albums, songs, plays, video games, artworks, newspapers, and journals. Render them in Roman type with initial capitals on all words except `a`, `an`, `and`, `at`, `for`, `from`, `in`, `of`, `on`, `the`, `to` (unless first word or after a colon): `The Handmaid's Tale`, `A Tale of Two Cities`, `There Is a Light That Never Goes Out`, `Superman: The Early Years`. Use lowercase `the` for newspapers and periodicals (`the Guardian`, `the Times`, `the New York Times`, `the Observer`). Use italics only for unassimilated foreign words/phrases (with Roman translation in brackets), poetry, and scientific names; never use italics in headlines or standfirsts.
      - **Punctuation**:
          - **Dashes**: Replace unspaced em-dashes or double hyphens with spaced en-dashes (`–`) for parenthetical pauses.
          - **Commas**: Remove Oxford (serial) commas in straightforward lists (`ham, eggs and chips`), retaining them only where needed to prevent ambiguity or group complex items (`cereal, kippers, bacon, eggs, toast and marmalade, and tea`).
          - **Quotation Marks & Colons**: Use double quotation marks (`"..."`) for body-copy speech, primary quotes, and scare quotes; use single quotation marks (`'...'`) for quotes within quotes, headlines, standfirsts, and captions. Use a colon (not a comma) to introduce a full-sentence quotation (`He said: "You'll never take me alive."`). Place final full stops and commas inside quotation marks if the quoted matter is a complete sentence (or ends with a complete sentence), and outside if it is a sentence fragment. Use square brackets `[...]` for parenthetical insertions inside direct quotes.
          - **Quote Preservation vs House Rules**: Inside direct quotations (`"..."`), strictly preserve the speaker's original wording, dialect, syntax, and grammatical choices (`whilst`, `amidst`, `"an historic"`, colloquialisms), never altering or "improving" authentic speech. Apply Guardian rules only to typography and orthography (converting double hyphens to spaced en-dashes `–`, rendering referenced titles in Roman type without quotes or italics, standardising spelling like `color` -> `colour`, and ensuring proper double/single quote nesting).
          - **Apostrophes**: Use `'s` for singular possessives ending in `s` (`James's`, `Jones's`, `St James's Park`) unless pronunciation makes it awkward (`Waters'`, `Mephistopheles'`). Use apostrophes in time/measure phrases modifying a noun (`two days' time`, `12 years' imprisonment`, `six weeks' holiday`), but omit them when adverbial (`nine months pregnant`, `three weeks old`).
          - **Bullet Points**: End each bullet point with a full stop.
      - **Attribution and Reporting Verbs**: Default to neutral `said`. Avoid editorialising broadsheet/tabloid tags in body copy that subtly inject bias or doubt:
          - Replace `insisted` with `said` (unless persistent repetition under interrogation is the actual story).
          - Replace `admitted` with `said` or `acknowledged` (never use `admit` for neutral statements, sexuality, or non-criminal disclosures).
          - Replace `warned` with `said` unless a genuine hazard or safety danger is being communicated.
          - Avoid `commented`, `declared`, `claimed`, or `noted` when simple `said` will do.
          - *Reconciliation with author voice*: Preserving voice protects the author's ideas, rhythm, and prose style; it does not protect editorialising attribution tags or broadsheet clichés.
      - **Abbreviations and Acronyms**: Strip full stops and spaces from abbreviations and initials, including in proper names (`eg`, `ie`, `etc`, `am`, `pm`, `mph`, `Dr`, `Mr`, `MP`, `No 10`, `UK`, `US`, `AN Wilson`, `WH Smith`, `JRR Tolkien`). Use ALL CAPS for initialisms pronounced as individual letters (`BBC`, `CEO`, `NHS`, `API`, `FAQ`, `NGO`, `VAT`, plus `ICE` for US Immigration and Customs Enforcement), except `pdf` and `plc` which are always lowercase. Use initial capital only for acronyms pronounced as words (`Nasa`, `Nato`, `Ofcom`, `Unicef`, `Unesco`, `Covid`, `Aids`, `Defra`, `Cobra`, `Ukip`, `Cop29`), unless they have entered the language as everyday lowercase words (`awol`, `laser`, `asbo`, `sim card`, `pin`, `scuba`, `gif`). On first mention of an unfamiliar organisation used more than once, write the full name followed by the abbreviation in brackets.
      - **Numbers, Dates, Times, and Units**:
          - **General Numbers**: Spell out one to nine (`five books`, `third attempt`, `five minutes`, `two years`); use numerals from 10 to 999,999 (`10 years`, `11th hour`, `21st century`, `21st-century boy`). Spell out numbers starting a sentence.
          - **Millions, Billions, Trillions**: In body copy, spell out `million` and `billion` **only for people or animals** (`3 million people`, `25 million rabbits`); use `m`, `bn`, or `tn` with no space for **money, quantities, and inanimate objects** (`£5m`, `$10bn`, `45m tonnes of coal`, `30bn doses of vaccine`, `1bn litres of water`). Always use `m`, `bn`, `tn` in headlines.
          - **Ages**: Follow the one-to-nine rule and **never write "aged X"** after a name: write `Kamala Harris, 56,` (not `aged 56`), `Freddie Bawden Marsh, nine,`, `a five-year-old`, `a 62-year-old man`, `62 years old`, `in her 20s` (but `twentysomething`).
          - **Measurements & Temperatures**: Use numerals (including `1` to `9`) alongside units of measurement (`5 miles`, `3kg`, `10km`, `50kg`). Leave no space before abbreviated units (`10km`, `50kg`, `100cm`, `10kWh`, `20m`), and a space before spelled-out units (`5 miles`, `10 metres`). Use metric for weights and measures except `the mile` and `the pint` (and `hectares` first for land area, with `acres` in brackets). Format temperatures in Celsius with no degree symbol or space, giving Fahrenheit in brackets at first mention (`23C (73F)`, `-3C (27F)`).
          - **Percentages & Fractions**: Always use numerals with `%` (`5%`, `100%`). Distinguish `%` from `percentage points` (an increase from 3% to 5% is a `2 percentage point increase` or `2-point increase`, not a `2% increase`). Hyphenate simple fractions (`two-thirds`, `five-eighths`), but not mixed numbers (`two and a half`, `three and three-quarters`). Do not mix fractions and percentages when comparing figures.
          - **Dates, Decades, and Times**: Format dates as day month year with no commas or ordinals (`15 October 2026`, `21 July-6 August`, `2025-26` not `2025/26`). Place `AD` before the year (`AD64`) and `BC` after (`300BC`), but both after the century (`fourth century BC`, `second century AD`). Format decades without apostrophes (`1990s`, `the 60s`, `the 00s`). Format times using the 12-hour clock with a full stop and no space (`9.30am`, `4pm`), and always write `noon` and `midnight` (never `12 noon`, `12 midnight`, `12am`, or `12pm`).
          - **Temporal Markers (Digital vs Print)**: Avoid relative time words (`yesterday`, `today`, `tomorrow`) in digital copy where international readers access reporting across different time zones. In news copy, convert or flag them, specifying the explicit day of the week instead (eg `on Wednesday`).
4.  **Apply Verb Agreement, House Terminology, and A-Z Rules**:
      - **Verb Agreement**: Company names and political parties take singular verbs (`Marks & Spencer is`, `Labour is promising`, `the Conservative party is`, though `the Conservatives are`). Sports teams take plural verbs (`Arsenal are`, `England have won`). Collective nouns (`government`, `cabinet`, `committee`, `family`, `jury`, `squad`, `staff`) take singular or plural depending on whether treated as a unit or individuals, keeping verb and pronoun consistent within the sentence.
      - **Climate & Environment**: Prefer `climate emergency` or `climate crisis` (or `climate breakdown` in scientific contexts) over `climate change` when describing the broader crisis; `global heating` over `global warming`; `climate science denier` or `climate denier` over `climate sceptic`; `wildlife` over `biodiversity`; `fish populations` over `fish stocks`; and `greenhouse gas emissions` over `carbon emissions` when covering all warming gases.
      - **Inclusive & People-First Terms**: Use gender-neutral occupational nouns (`actor` for all genders except in award names like `Oscar for best actress`, `chair`, `spokesperson`, `firefighter`, `police officer`, `flight attendant`/`cabin crew`, `headteacher`, `humankind`/`humanity`). Use `disabled people` (not `the disabled`), `uses a wheelchair` (not `wheelchair-bound`), `people with Aids` (not `Aids victims`), `black` and `Asian` lowercase as adjectives (`black people`, not `blacks`), `Indigenous` and `Aboriginal` capitalised as adjectives (never `Aborigine`), and `antisemitic`/`antisemitism` (one word, lowercase, no hyphen).
5.  **Output Format**:
      - Present the **Edited Text** first in a clean, ready-to-use block.
      - Follow with a concise **Sub-Editor's Changelog** bulleting the specific categories of changes applied (with a full stop at the end of each bullet, per Guardian style). If the text required zero changes, state that it already conforms to Guardian style.
